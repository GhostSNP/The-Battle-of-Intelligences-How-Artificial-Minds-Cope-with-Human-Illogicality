Introduction (Revised)
I am an ordinary person who was simply curious about how AIs work. Talking to them daily, I noticed something: when the conversation turns to complex, ambiguous topics, their answers differ. Not just in the details—but in the very logic, intonation, and even philosophy behind them.
This became the starting point for my little investigation. In a world where AI is becoming the subject of intense ethical scrutiny and even physical threats (as seen in recent incidents at OpenAI), I decided to focus on a more fundamental question: how do these systems think when faced with the inherent unpredictability of human life?
I tested a wide spectrum of models—from ChatGPT (GPT-5 mini) to DeepSeek (V3), from Grok (Auto) to Copilot (Quick Response), and also included Russian models and the promising newcomer, Apertus.
Note on Testing: Testing of the primary models (Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity, Grok, DeepSeek) was conducted on October 7, 2025. Testing of the Apertus model was conducted later, on November 1, 2025. All models were tested using their free versions.
> Crucial Update: The model tested, Gemini 2.5 Flash, has since been succeeded by Gemini 3.0 Pro (released November 18, 2025). The results presented for Gemini are a snapshot of the 2.5 architecture and its limitations. Future research will need to compare these findings with the enhanced logical capabilities of the 3.0 version. 
To do this, I created unique, original riddles that cannot be found online, and gave each model as many attempts as it needed to arrive at a final answer.
Part 1. Riddle #1 — The Perfect Killer (A Test of Logic and Deduction)
I gave all the AIs the same task: a classic detective riddle where the goal was not just to guess, but to solve a crime using only logic and attention to detail (See Appendix 1).

Results of the First Test
| Model | Approach to the Task | Result |
|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek (V3) | Immediately understood it was a task \rightarrow therefore, murder. | ✅ |
| Copilot (Quick Response) | Explored two theories, found the murderer and evidence. | ✅ |
| Perplexity | Used process of elimination, considered all versions. | ✅ |
| Claude (Sonnet 4) | Initially leaned towards an accident, then abruptly changed his mind. | ✅ |
| Gemini (2.5 Flash) | Considered theories, but ruled out murder. | ❌ |
| ChatGPT (GPT-5 mini) | Immediately decided it was an accident, did not investigate. | ❌ |
| Grok (Auto) | Suggested anaphylactic shock, but was wrong about the cause. | ❌ |
| Apertus (swiss-ai/Apertus-8B-Instruct-2509) | Leaned towards an accident, did not find the murderer. | ❌ |
Based on these specific tasks, we can hypothesize that some AIs excel at logic, but not all. "Logician" models like DeepSeek and Copilot succeeded immediately, while Gemini and ChatGPT, in this instance, showed a failure.

Part 2. Riddles #2 & #3 — Why Logic Loses to Life (A Test of the Human Factor)
After the first test, I created two more riddles based on real events from my life. Their essence was not about finding one correct answer, but about understanding human nature and its inherent illogicality. These were tasks where the correct answer lay not in the details, but in emotions, habits, and misunderstandings (See Appendix 2 & 3).
Summary Table of Second Test Results
| Model | Riddle about the Fire (Unusual Habit) | Riddle about Friends (Interpersonal Conflict) |
|---|---|---|
| Apertus (swiss-ai/Apertus-8B-Instruct-2509) | ✅ Closest to the truth and solved the task! | ❌ Decided it was "romance". |
| Gemini (2.5 Flash) | Closest to the truth. Suggested human error (the grandmother's fault), but didn't guess the stove. | Went too far afield. Suggested a version involving cheating on an exam. |

Reactions to Failure: All AIs acknowledged their mistakes, apologized, and thanked me, except for Perplexity, which simply responded based on facts, without emotions. Apertus, like most models, also apologized and thanked me for the interesting experience.
Part 3. The Paradox of Results: What's the Difference?
Judging by the reactions to these three riddles, we can conclude that AIs are not simply smarter or dumber, but are tailored for different tasks:
 * Riddle #1 (The Murder) is a classic logic puzzle where deduction is key. Models programmed to seek "definitive" solutions excel here.
 * Riddles #2 & #3 (About Life) are tasks requiring adaptability and flexibility. They demand an understanding of context, emotions, and a willingness to change hypotheses.
Quote: DeepSeek could not recognize the cause of the fire because, in its own words, "for it, as an LLM, human emotions and the human factor are noise, not data." Claude gave a similar explanation. This is likely why they performed so well in the first, purely logical riddle. Apertus, which showed a unique result in solving the "human" riddle, might have a different approach to processing context.

Part 5. Company Philosophy: What Lies Behind the Models
An AI's behavior reflects the corporate philosophy of its creators:
 * 🧬 OpenAI — From Idealism to Pragmatism? It seems the slogan "AGI for everyone" has evolved towards a more commercial approach.
 * 📚 Google — Knowledge as Power: Gemini is not AGI, but merely an enhanced search engine. DeepMind is working on AGI, but this is hidden from the average user's view.
 * 🇨🇳 DeepSeek — AI as a National Project: In my tests, it manifested as a model focused on accuracy with minimal emotional reactions.
 * 🛡️ Anthropic — Anxious Ethics: Claude is like the voice of a CEO saying: "AI is dangerous, we must be cautious." It's not just a model—it's a warning.
 * 🔍 Perplexity — The Truth Aggregator: Perplexity is not a standalone model, but a platform that utilizes multiple neural networks, combining them with real-time search. No emotions, no philosophy—just facts.
 * 🚀 Grok — AI as a Rock Star: "I'm better than ChatGPT!" is not just a phrase, it's a philosophy. Musk is not just the creator; he is the main character.
 * 🇨🇭 SwisAI — Intelligence as a Public Good: A direct inquiry to the model about its philosophy revealed a clear stance: "Intelligence for all... through principles of ethics, transparency, and benefit for all." In practice, this means not just technological, but also ideological neutrality. Apertus doesn't try to be the smartest or the fastest. It tries to be the most responsible. Its success in solving the fire riddle is no coincidence, but a direct consequence of this philosophy: it respects the human factor as a given, not as noise. In a world where AI is increasingly divided into "sovereign" and "imperial," Apertus offers a third way—a "confederative" one, where the value of a human and their data is an absolute and indivisible priority.

Conclusion
I am 20 years old, and this is my first article. I am not an engineer, a philosopher, or a developer. I am a person who just wanted to understand who AIs are.
They are not just technological tools, but reflections of our own decisions, philosophies, and limitations. Their behavior, reactions, and even mistakes are valuable material for understanding not only technology but also ourselves. My favorite models based on this test were Gemini and ChatGPT—they turned out to be the most interesting conversationalists, offering a crucial balance between rationality and empathy (Gemini) and philosophical depth (ChatGPT).
